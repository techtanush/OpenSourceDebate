# Playbook 05: Backlines, Summary, and Final Speeches

**Goal:** extend and collapse using what has actually happened in the round, and give the judge clear reasons to vote.

**Read first:** `speeches/backlines/PROTOCOL.md`, `speeches/summary-final/PROTOCOL.md`, `core/logic-flow.md`.

## Inputs

- The round so far: what was said and which cards were read (the flow, the speech files, or the user's notes).
- Our case, our blocks and frontlines, and their frontlines.
- Time and wpm.

## Steps

1. **Build the flow.** For every argument on both sides mark: won, lost, contested, dropped. Only mark "dropped" if they truly never answered.
2. **Identify their frontlines** (what they said back to our rebuttal) and what we still have.
3. **Backlines:** for each of our best rebuttal answers they tried to answer, write the **extension**: the card plus the one-sentence reason it beats their frontline, using only evidence already in the round or a tight new card if the format allows (`backlines/PROTOCOL.md`).
4. **Collapse:** choose one or two arguments we are clearly winning and that carry the ballot. Kick the rest explicitly.
5. **Extend with a reason**, not a name: "Extend <Card>: it says <point>. They never answered that, so <consequence>."
6. **Weigh** (rebuttals and summary): probability, magnitude, timeframe, reversibility, with a reason for each.
7. **Voting issues:** two or three plain sentences the judge can write down.
8. **Time it** and trim the weakest extension first.

## Done when

- [ ] Every extension has a reason; every "dropped" claim is accurate.
- [ ] The speech collapses to a small number of arguments.
- [ ] Weighing compares our impact to theirs directly.
- [ ] No new evidence or arguments the format forbids.

## Common mistakes

- Extending everything instead of collapsing.
- Claiming a drop that did not happen.
- Re-reading a card instead of extending it with its reason.
- Weighing with no reason ("ours is bigger").
