Downtown Traffic and Injury Trends, 2019 to 2024

By the Larkspur Office of Transportation Planning, published February 2025.

Daily private-car entries to the Larkspur downtown core rose 17 percent between 2019 and 2024, from roughly 61,000 to 71,000 vehicles per day. Over the same period, pedestrian and cyclist injuries in the core rose 22 percent, from 338 to 412 per year. The office's forecast model projects a further 9 percent increase in car entries by 2030 if current development plans proceed without changes to street access.

Planners attribute the growth to new housing and office space within a short drive of downtown, combined with free curbside parking in much of the core. Transit ridership downtown has been flat over the same period.

The office cautions that its forecast depends on continued job growth in the district, and that a recession could slow the increase. Even in its low scenario, however, it projects car entries rising 4 percent by 2030.

The office says it will publish updated counts each January and has asked the city council to fund a second set of sensors on the streets that border the downtown core, so that changes in traffic patterns can be tracked more closely over the coming years.
