Fairhaven's Downtown Car Restriction: Three-Year Results

By the Fairhaven Department of Public Works, evaluation report, June 2025.

Fairhaven, a city of 190,000, restricted ordinary private cars from a 0.8-square-mile downtown core in 2021, after an 18-month phase-in with expanded bus service, a permit system for disabled residents and deliveries, and free transfers at edge parking. The department tracked injuries, traffic, and complaints for three years.

Pedestrian and cyclist injuries in the core fell from 96 in 2020 to 58 in 2024, a decline of 40 percent, while citywide injuries were roughly flat. Traffic counts on the eight streets bordering the core changed by less than 3 percent. Bus ridership into downtown rose 26 percent.

The department reports that accessibility complaints fell sharply after the first year, as permit processing was streamlined and the city added two accessible drop-off points. "The first six months were rough," said public works director Helen Park. "The phase-in and the permits are what made it work. Cities that skip them will have problems."
