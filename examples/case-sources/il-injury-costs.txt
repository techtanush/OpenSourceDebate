The Cost of a Serious Pedestrian Injury

By the Meridian Health Economics Group, Policy Brief No. 14, October 2024.

Pedestrian injuries are usually discussed as counts of people hurt rather than as costs borne by patients, families, and public budgets, and the brief was written to fill that gap. A serious pedestrian or cyclist injury, defined as one requiring hospital admission, carries an average total cost of about $187,000 when medical care, rehabilitation, and lost earnings are counted. For the roughly one in six serious injuries that result in permanent impairment, the lifetime cost rises above $1.1 million.

Hospital care accounts for just under half of the cost. The remainder is split between rehabilitation and long-term care and the wages lost by the injured person and by family members who step away from work to provide care. Public insurance programs and hospitals absorb a large share of the cost; in the cities studied, about 41 percent was paid by public programs.

The brief estimates that each serious injury prevented therefore saves, on average, between $75,000 in public spending and the full $187,000 in total social cost, depending on whether private costs are included.

The authors caution that cost estimates vary with how lost earnings and long-term care are valued, and that the figures here use the median of the ranges reported in the studies they reviewed.
