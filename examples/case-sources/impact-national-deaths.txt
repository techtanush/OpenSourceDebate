Pedestrian and Cyclist Fatalities in Urban Areas

By the National Example Safety Council, Annual Statistical Report, 2024 edition.

The council's annual report compiles police crash records from every state and adjusts them using hospital data to correct for undercounting. Across the country's urban areas, 7,310 pedestrians and cyclists were killed in traffic crashes in 2023, the highest annual toll in more than four decades. Another 71,000 suffered serious injuries requiring hospital admission. Deaths among people walking rose 44 percent over the preceding ten years, while deaths among vehicle occupants rose 10 percent.

More than half of these deaths occurred in dense urban cores and arterial streets where pedestrians and vehicles share space. The council estimates that the economic cost of these crashes, including medical care, lost productivity, and quality-of-life valuation, exceeded $140 billion in 2023.

The council notes that the deaths are concentrated among people who have little choice about their exposure: children walking to school, older adults crossing at intersections, and workers commuting on foot or by bike. "These are people who are killed doing ordinary things," said council president Daniel Okafor.
