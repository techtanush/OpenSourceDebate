A new analysis from the Example Transit Institute finds that cities which closed their downtown cores to private cars saw __traffic fall sharply on surrounding streets__ and no measurable loss in retail sales. ... "Merchants predicted disaster," Reyes said. "What we measured was the opposite. Foot traffic rose, and total sales were flat to slightly up in eleven of the twelve cities." ==Average travel times on the streets bordering each ban zone fell by nine percent==, because drivers switched to transit or took different routes before ever reaching downtown.

> Nine percent faster bordering streets means displacement is not happening.
