Ashford Pedestrian Pilot Cut Downtown Sales, Merchants Say

By Tom Reyes, Business Reporter, The Example Business Journal. Published May 20, 2024.

Downtown merchants in Ashford say sales fell by 18 percent in the first four months after the city closed four downtown streets to cars as part of a pedestrian pilot. Owners of eleven shops told the Journal that regular customers stopped coming and that deliveries became harder to schedule. The Ashford Chamber of Commerce, which surveyed its members, called the pilot "a warning to every city considering a car ban."

The pilot covered only four blocks of the downtown, and the city made no changes to bus service while it ran. Several merchants said the lack of transit made the drop worse, and some noted that parking garages at the edge of the pilot area stayed open and were well used.

By late summer, sales on the pilot streets had recovered to within three percent of their pre-pilot level, according to city sales-tax receipts reviewed by the Journal. The chamber said it would continue to oppose permanent closures until the city provided a plan for deliveries.

City officials said they plan to add a bus route and a delivery window before any permanent decision. "We learned a lot," said Mayor Elena Park. "The lesson is that you can't just close the street and walk away."
