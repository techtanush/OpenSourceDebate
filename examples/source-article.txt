Downtown Car Bans Cut Congestion Without Hurting Retail, Study Finds

By Dana Whitfield, Staff Writer, The Example Urban Review. Published March 3, 2025.

A new analysis from the Example Transit Institute finds that cities which closed their downtown cores to private cars saw traffic fall sharply on surrounding streets and no measurable loss in retail sales. The study, led by transportation economist Dr. Marcus Reyes, followed twelve mid-sized cities for four years after their bans took effect.

"Merchants predicted disaster," Reyes said. "What we measured was the opposite. Foot traffic rose, and total sales were flat to slightly up in eleven of the twelve cities." Average travel times on the streets bordering each ban zone fell by nine percent, because drivers switched to transit or took different routes before ever reaching downtown.

The report cautions that results depend on transit quality. In the one city where sales fell, buses ran only every forty minutes. "A ban without a working alternative just moves the problem," Reyes said. Cities that paired bans with frequent service saw the largest gains in both ridership and retail activity.

The institute recommends that cities phase in restrictions over two years and publish yearly data so residents can judge results for themselves.
