# Backlines Protocol

**Backlines** are the extensions of **our rebuttal** against the opponent's **frontlines**. They are usually built in the round from what the opponent actually said, so they are rarely fully pre-written. This protocol covers both live use and light prep.

Not to be confused with: blocks (attacks on their case) and frontlines (defense of our own case). See `core/file-organization.md`.

Read first: `core/integrity.md`, `core/refutation-guide.md`, `core/logic-flow.md`. Procedure: `playbooks/05-backlines-and-summary.md`.

## When they are used

After you answered an argument (blocks) and the opponent defended it (their frontline), you extend your answer by showing why it still stands against what they said.

## What a backline contains

For each of our rebuttal answers they tried to answer:

```
Our answer (the one they responded to)
Their response (stated fairly, one sentence)
Our extension: the card (already read, so an Extend analytic) + the one-sentence reason it beats their response
If new evidence is allowed and needed: one tight new card
```

## Process

1. **List our rebuttal answers** and mark which ones they answered, which they dropped, and which they only partly answered.
2. **For each answered one, write their response in one fair sentence.** If you can't, you don't have it from the round; ask the user.
3. **Choose the extension type:**
   - **They didn't answer the card itself:** "Extend <Card>: it says <point>. Their response was about <something else>."
   - **They answered with a weaker claim:** show the scope, date, or method gap in their response (`refutation-guide.md`, hole checklist).
   - **They conceded part:** hold them to it and extend the rest.
   - **They turned it:** answer the turn directly with the card that shows the mechanism goes our way.
4. **Add a card only when needed** and allowed by the format; cut it with context and verify it.
5. **Rank** the extensions by importance to the ballot; you will collapse in the next speech.
6. **Keep them short.** A backline extension should take 15-30 seconds.

## Light prep (before the round)

If asked for prep, prepare **extension cards** for the two or three rebuttal answers most likely to be contested: the card, a one-sentence "if they say X, extend this because Y," and a backup card. Do not write a full document; the round will decide what is needed.

## Rules

- **Extend, don't re-read.** Cards already read are extended with their key point and the reason.
- **Only claim a drop that really happened.**
- **Don't introduce new arguments** the format forbids at this stage.
- **Be consistent** with everything said earlier in the round.

## Quality checks

- [ ] Each extension names the card, the point, and the reason it beats their response.
- [ ] Their response is stated fairly.
- [ ] Drops claimed are accurate.
- [ ] Any new card is verified and allowed by the format.
