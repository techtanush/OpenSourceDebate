# Cross-Examination Protocol

Used for: preparing questions for cross-ex / crossfire, and building question banks against a known case.

Read first: `core/integrity.md` (questions must quote their evidence accurately).

## Goals of cross-ex

1. **Get admissions** you can use in your next speech ("In cross-ex they agreed that...").
2. **Expose contradictions** between their cards or between their positions.
3. **Clarify** what they are actually claiming so you answer the real argument.
4. **Set up** a later answer without revealing it.

Cross-ex is **not** the place to argue. Ask, get the answer, move on.

## Process

1. **Read their whole case** (text or file) and list each argument with its cards and cites.
2. **Find the openings** (look for these in each card):
   - **Scope:** the card is about something narrower or different than their tag.
   - **Date:** the card is old for a fast-moving claim.
   - **Source:** bias, credentials, location, type of publication.
   - **Their own words:** a qualifier, concession, or section title that undercuts them.
   - **Cross-position contradictions:** two cards or arguments that can't both be true.
   - **Mechanism gaps:** a step in the causal chain with no evidence.
3. **Write questions** (below), each tied to a specific card and what you want from the answer.
4. **Rank** them. Put the most valuable five at the top for a short cross-ex.
5. **Plan the follow-up** in your next speech for each: "They admitted X; that means Y."

## How to write a good question

- **Closed-ended:** the answer is "yes," "no," or a short fact.
- **One idea per question.**
- **Based on their evidence**, quoted accurately. Use the exact words from the card.
- **Safe:** you already know the likely answer, or both answers help you.
- **Short.** If it's more than two sentences, split it.

### Patterns

| Pattern | Example shape |
|---------|---------------|
| Scope | "Your [Author] card is about X. Where does it mention Y?" |
| Quote back | "Your [Author] card says '[exact words].' Doesn't that mean...?" |
| Date | "Your [Author] card is from [year], before [event]. What does it say about [event]?" |
| Source | "What is [Author]'s expertise on [topic]?" / "Who funds [organization]?" |
| Contradiction | "Your [A] card says [x] and your [B] card says [y]. Which is true?" |
| Mechanism | "Walk me through how [step 1] leads to [step 3]. Which card shows the middle step?" |
| Scale | "How many [units] does the resolution affect, out of how many in your evidence?" |

## Output

A short list organized by their argument, each line: the question, the card it targets, and the use ("sets up: no link"). Then a "top 5 if you only have a minute" list and a note on what to say in the next speech.

## Quality checks

- [ ] Every quote about their evidence matches their card word for word.
- [ ] Questions are closed-ended and one idea each.
- [ ] Each question has a purpose in your next speech.
- [ ] No questions that give them a chance to make a speech.
- [ ] You flagged any card text you could not read (e.g. only a snippet).

Be fair: do not misstate what a card says. A misquoted question damages your credibility.
